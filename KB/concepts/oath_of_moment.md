# Oath of Moment

The Space Marine army rule: each turn the player nominates one enemy unit, and the army attacks that unit better. It turns target selection into the faction's defining decision.

## The shape of the mechanic

At the start of a turn the Space Marine player names one enemy unit as the target of the Oath. For the rest of that turn, Space Marine units attacking that unit get a benefit - historically a re-roll on hit rolls, sometimes extending to wound rolls.

The nomination is made once, applies army-wide, and cannot generally be changed mid-turn. Whether any of that survives into 11th Edition is exactly what needs verifying.

---

## Why it is good to learn on

This is a genuinely well-shaped rule for a beginner, and it is worth being explicit about why - it is the reason [[space_marines]] is the right second army for the son rather than just the available one.

- **One decision, whole army.** Everything the army does this turn keys off a single choice. A new player can hold that in their head; they cannot yet hold six conditional stratagems.
- **Immediate, legible feedback.** Nominate the wrong unit and the turn visibly underperforms. Nominate the right one and it visibly works. The lesson arrives in the same turn as the decision, which is the fastest way anyone learns a game.
- **It teaches target priority, which transfers.** Deciding *what to kill first* is the skill that carries into every army and every wargame. Oath of Moment forces the question out loud every single turn instead of letting it stay implicit.

The failure mode to teach against: nominating whatever is closest or most threatening-looking, rather than whatever most needs removing to win the objective game. A dangerous unit that is not contesting anything is often the wrong Oath target.

## The teaching contrast with Necrons

The two army rules in this project sit at opposite ends of the same axis, and putting them side by side is more instructive than either alone.

| | [[oath_of_moment]] | [[reanimation_protocols]] |
|---|---|---|
| Plane | Offence - concentrate damage | Defence - refuse to stay damaged |
| Decision | One explicit nomination per turn | Continuous positional judgement |
| Skill taught | Target priority | Board presence and patience |
| Feedback | Immediate, same turn | Delayed, over several turns |
| Failure mode | Oathing the wrong unit | Retreating a unit that would have survived |

Against Necrons specifically, Oath of Moment is the answer to the attrition problem: the Necron player is rewarded for making the opponent spread damage thin, and Oath of Moment exists to concentrate it. **Naming a Necron unit and removing it entirely beats damaging two of them**, because whatever is not killed comes back. That single interaction is probably the most valuable thing to teach both players early.
