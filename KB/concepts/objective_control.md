# Objective Control

A characteristic on every model's profile representing how strongly it holds ground. Totalling it decides who **controls** an objective — and controlling objectives is how games are won. Core chapter **14**.

---

## The mechanic (14.01 / 14.02)

Most missions put each objective on a defined **terrain area** (**14.01**). That terrain **footprint** *is* the objective. A model is within range while it is **inside that terrain area** (on / touching the footprint). Where a mission uses a flat **objective marker** instead, range is typically **3" horizontally / 5" vertically**.

Each model has an **Objective Control** characteristic, usually written **OC**. At the end of **every phase and every turn** (**14.02**):

1. Each player **sums the OC** of all their eligible models within range of that objective.
2. The **higher total** controls it. A **tie** means nobody controls it (unless the objective is **Secured**).

**Control vs scoring:** **14.02** decides *who holds*. **Victory Points** come from the **mission card** — score when it says for objectives you currently control.

## Consequences

- **Control flips constantly.** Re-checked every phase — walk onto the footprint and you can take an objective without firing.
- **Bodies beat quality.** Ten models with OC 1 out-hold one tank with OC 3.
- **Battle-shocked units contribute 0 OC.** Morale can hand over an objective you thought was safe.
- **Secured** objectives can stay yours after models leave until the opponent out-controls you at the end of a phase.

---

## Why beginners lose to this

Modern 40K is scored on objectives, not kills. Removing more of the opponent's army and still losing is common: killing is visible; standing on a footprint is not.

Downstream of this page: [[reanimation_protocols]], [[power_matrix]], [[cryptek_conclave]], [[oath_of_moment]].

**Habit:** before moving, ask which footprints are contested and what would flip the OC totals.
