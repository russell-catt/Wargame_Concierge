# Glossary

The single home for **Keyword** entries and every game term this KB uses. Check here before using a rules term anywhere else. Updated on every ingest that introduces or refines terminology.

Keywords do **not** get their own pages - see [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md) Sec 5 for the rule and the promotion test that moves a term into `KB/concepts/`.

## Terminology

**Objective Control** *(OC)* - `unverified`
: A characteristic on a model's profile representing how strongly it holds ground. To decide who controls an objective marker, each player totals the OC of their eligible models within range of it; the higher total controls the objective. It is the reason a cheap, numerous unit can hold ground that an elite unit cannot, and it is why 40K is won by positioning rather than by kill count.
- Faction / scope: core rules, system-wide
- Verify against: 11e core rules - objective control and scoring section
- Open: whether 11e changed how OC is calculated, and how abilities that modify or ignore OC now work

**Reanimation Protocols** - `unverified`
: The Necron army-wide resilience mechanic. Necron units recover from damage over the course of the game, returning destroyed models or restoring lost wounds at a defined point in the turn sequence. Tactically it is the core of the faction: it rewards keeping units on objectives and makes an opponent's incomplete damage output much less valuable, so understanding exactly *when* it triggers and *what* it restores is the first thing a Necron player must learn.
- Faction / scope: Necrons, army rule
- Verify against: 11e Necrons army rules; cross-check on Wahapedia
- Open: the 11e trigger timing, how much is restored, and what interrupts or denies it
- See also: [[necrons]] *(page not yet created)*

**Oath of Moment** - `unverified`
: The Space Marine army-wide rule. Each turn the Space Marine player nominates one enemy unit as the target of the Oath, and Space Marine units gain an accuracy or re-roll benefit when attacking that unit. It concentrates the army's output onto one target per turn, which makes target selection the defining Space Marine decision and rewards planning a turn ahead.
- Faction / scope: Space Marines, army rule
- Verify against: 11e Space Marines army rules; cross-check on Wahapedia
- Open: the exact 11e benefit, when the target is nominated, and whether it can change mid-turn
- See also: [[space_marines]] *(page not yet created)*

**Power Matrix** - `unverified`
: A Necron / Cryptek resource mechanic carried into this KB from the owner's existing Necron notes, associated with the **Hierotek Circle** set. The general shape is a pool of arcane power that Cryptek models generate and spend to fuel abilities during the game.
: **Scope is genuinely unconfirmed.** The Hierotek Circle is a Kill Team box, and this term may belong to *Kill Team* rules rather than to Warhammer 40,000 11th Edition. Resolve which game system owns this term before writing any 40K content that depends on it - if it turns out to be Kill Team only, it should be relabelled or removed rather than quietly carried forward.
- Faction / scope: Necrons / Cryptek - **system attribution unresolved (40K vs Kill Team)**
- Verify against: Hierotek Circle set contents once photo ID is complete; then 11e Necrons rules
- Open: which game system this belongs to; whether any 40K 11e equivalent exists
- See also: the Hierotek Circle photo-ID thread in [[overview]]

## Style conventions

How this KB writes about the game.

| Convention | Rule | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Edition naming | Write "11th Edition" in prose, `warhammer_40k_11e` in frontmatter and paths | "new in 11th Edition" |
| Official term first | Use the official term, then the community shorthand once in parentheses | "Objective Control (OC)" |
| No verbatim rules text | Paraphrase for teaching; never transcribe datasheet or stratagem wording | see [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md) Sec 10 |
| Cite the check | Every rules claim names where it can be verified, with a retrieval date | "Wahapedia, read 2026-08-16" |
| Beginner voice | Explain why a rule matters, not just what it says | - |
