# Canoptek Court

The Necron detachment built around robotic constructs, whose detachment rule is the **Power Matrix** - hit re-rolls for units operating in territory the Necron player controls.

---

## The detachment rule: Power Matrix

**This is the 40K rule that L0 could not place.** [[glossary]] originally warned that "Power Matrix" might be a Kill Team term. It is not: it is this detachment's rule in Warhammer 40,000, confirmed by two independent in-repo sources. The full correction is on [[power_matrix]].

What it does, in the owner's paraphrase: units within controlled territory re-roll their hit rolls. Two consequences follow if that shape is right.

- **It rewards holding ground rather than raiding it.** A re-roll you only get inside your own zone means the shooting is best when the army is standing where the objectives are - which is where a Necron army wants to be anyway.
- **It couples offence to [[objective_control]].** Territory control is not just the scoring mechanism, it becomes the damage mechanism. Losing a zone costs points *and* accuracy in the same turn.

The rule's **name and ownership are settled**; its **wording is not**. "Controlled territory" could mean a deployment zone, an objective's range, or something 11e-specific. Whether the re-roll covers melee, shooting, or both is unknown. Verify against the Necrons faction pack at `raw/pointers/faction_pack_necrons.md` before playing it.

---

## What the detachment wants

Canoptek constructs: **Canoptek Wraiths** and **Canoptek Doomstalkers** are the named centrepieces, with Scarab Swarms and Warriors screening.

The plan described by [[necron_lists_owner_notes]] is aggressive midfield pressure supported by backline artillery. Wraiths push into a central objective specifically to *open up* a Power Matrix zone in No Man's Land, so the rest of the army gets its re-rolls in the middle of the board rather than at home. Doomstalkers sit back and shoot into that contested ground.

That is a coherent gameplan and a demanding one - it asks a beginner to fight for the middle of the table on turn one or two.
