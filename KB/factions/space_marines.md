# Space Marines

The opposing army, played by the owner's son: a straightforward, forgiving faction built from existing older kits, used to teach contrast against the Necrons' attrition game.

## Role in this project

Space Marines exist here to make the Necrons legible. A beginner cannot tell that "your models come back" is unusual without something normal to compare it against, and Space Marines are the game's reference point: reasonable durability, reasonable output, few special cases.

Two facts shape the content that gets written:

- **The son is the player.** Content should be teachable to a second person who is learning at the same time, not just usable as an opponent profile.
- **The models are existing old kits.** Legacy and Firstborn datasheets stay in scope. This is not a curated modern collection, and advice that assumes current-range models will not match what is in the box.

## Army rule: Oath of Moment

Each turn the Space Marine player nominates one enemy unit, and the army attacks it better. See [[oath_of_moment]].

Why it matters for teaching: it forces a **single explicit decision per turn** with visible consequences. For a new player that is close to ideal - the whole army's plan compresses into one choice, and the feedback is immediate. It also sets up the natural contrast with [[reanimation_protocols]], where the Necron player's plan is spread across positioning rather than concentrated in a nomination.

`unverified` in 11th Edition wording.

## Detachment: Gladius Task Force

The named learning target, recorded in `games/warhammer_40k_11e/armies/space_marines/README.md` and in the project's initial requirements. See [[gladius_task_force]].

It is the generalist Space Marine detachment - the one that asks least of a list and is hardest to build badly. That is the right property for a second player's first army, independent of how it ranks competitively.
