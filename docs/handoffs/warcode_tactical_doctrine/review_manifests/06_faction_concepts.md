## The studio's stated identities

| Faction | Concept | Tags | Stated difficulty |
|---------|---------|------|-------------------|
| **MDR** | Clones of a single commander; "one strategic mind distributed across eight bodies"; synchronisation over durability; unit position drives what the squad can do next | Coordinated, Precise, Adaptable | Mid `[PL §02]` |
| **Ulfari** | Outside-system aliens; close distance fast, hit before the opponent reacts; average armour, high agility; melee-first | Aggressive, Fast, Melee-focused | Beginner-friendly `[PL §02]` |
| **Protagen** | Engineering faction in heavy suits; trade mobility for resilience; take a position and hold it | Durable, Methodical, Punishing | Mid / beginner-friendly `[PL §02]` |
| **Dominium** | Elite enforcement wing; one unit degrades nearby enemy morale, another amplifies allies; combat + support + psychological control at once | Flexible, Combination-based, Deep mastery | Expert `[PL §02]` |

All four rows: confidence **draft** (marketing copy, unverified against rules).

## Concept-to-execution check (the two playable factions)

### Protagen — concept mostly holds
- "Durable": armour sits a step above Ulfari across the roster; two units are slow. Matches "trade mobility for resilience." `[RB pp.33–34]` — confidence: draft
- "Methodical": shotgun-heavy roster with short reach pushes them toward advancing behind cover rather than trading at range. `[RB pp.7, 33–34]` — confidence: draft
- "Punishing": Smasher's forced-Melee-Lock rider genuinely punishes anyone who walks into 1". `[RB pp.15, 34]` — confidence: verified
- **Tension:** Protagen also field the roster's highest melee-attack unit and its hardest-hitting melee weapon, which cuts against Ulfari being "most dangerous in melee." `[RB pp.34, 35–36]` — confidence: draft

### Ulfari — concept partially holds
- "Fast": two units above baseline movement, versus none in Protagen. Holds. `[RB pp.35–36]` — confidence: draft
- "High agility": whole roster a step harder to hit, one unit at the cap. Holds. `[RB pp.35–36]` — confidence: draft
- "Average armor": in practice **below** Protagen across the board. "Average" is generous framing with only two rosters published. `[RB pp.33–36]` — confidence: draft
- "Melee-focused": supported by a melee-only unit and armour-cutting blades, but **not** by raw attack volume — see the Protagen tension above. — confidence: draft
- "Beginner-friendly": defensible. The plan is legible (close, engage), and Engage costing a full 2 AP means fewer decisions per activation. `[RB p.14]` — confidence: draft

### MDR — unverifiable, but the mechanical hook exists
- Concept needs AP-sharing and positional dependency. The core rules already reserve a slot for exactly that: "Some units have abilities that give extra AP to another friendly unit." `[RB p.3]` — confidence: verified (rule exists), unverified (MDR uses it)
- **Neither published roster contains such a unit** → strongly suggests the rule was written for MDR. — confidence: draft (inference)

### Dominium — unverifiable, and one flag
- Concept promises **morale degradation** and **ally amplification**. `[PL §02]` — confidence: draft
- **The beta rulebook has no morale, suppression, pinning, or fear system of any kind** in the text read. Either Dominium introduces a whole subsystem, or "morale" is marketing shorthand for a stat debuff. Worth a direct VIP question. `[RB pp.1–37]` — confidence: verified (absence)
- "Most mechanically complex squad" + "rewards planning several activations ahead" sits awkwardly with a 2-AP, 4-round game — there are few activations to plan across. — confidence: draft (inference)

## Difficulty-curve read

- The studio publishes a genuine ramp: Beginner (Ulfari) → Mid-Beginner (Protagen) → Mid (MDR) → Expert (Dominium). `[PL §02]` — confidence: draft
- Publishing difficulty ratings **before** launch is unusually player-friendly and gives new players a defensible first pick. — confidence: draft
- Risk: the two beginner factions are the two shipped in the beta, so the beta cannot demonstrate the top of the curve. — confidence: verified

## What the rules give every faction regardless of concept

These are chassis-level, not faction-level, and constrain how different any faction can feel `[RB pp.3, 4, 16, 22, 23]` — confidence: verified:

- 2 AP per unit; 8 units; alternating activation
- 4 shared equipment points, spendable only on grenades and medkits
- The same Leader re-roll economy and the same death-driven re-roll income
- The same contract catch-up mechanism
- The same Overwatch, cover, and Melee Lock rules

**Implication for the review:** faction identity in The Warcode is expressed almost entirely through statlines and a handful of one-line weapon riders. A reader coming from a game with faction-wide army rules, strategem decks, and detachment packages should expect a much thinner differentiation layer. Whether Protocol Cards change that is unknown — five unread image pages. `[RB pp.28–32]` — confidence: draft
