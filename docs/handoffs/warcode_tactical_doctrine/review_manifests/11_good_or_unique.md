# Manifest §11 — What's Good or Unique

- **Track:** warcode_tactical_doctrine
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-23
- **Citation legend:** see `00_what_this_is_not.md`
- **Standard applied:** "good" = the rule does real work at the table. "Unique" = not standard practice in small-squad skirmish games. Claims about the wider market are `draft` inference; claims about Warcode's own rules are `verified` from the beta.

## Genuinely strong mechanics

### Cover is offensive as well as defensive
- A shooter within 1" of a piece of partial cover **counts as being behind it** and shoots without interference from that piece. `[RB p.12]` — confidence: verified
- Cover **on the line of fire** counts, not just cover touching the target, and each piece stacks — up to the agility cap of 5. `[RB p.11]` — confidence: verified
- **Why it is good:** it makes the whole firing lane a puzzle rather than a yes/no visibility check, and it rewards hugging terrain on the way in. — confidence: draft

### Grenades are a designed answer to a designed problem
- Grenades skip the hit roll entirely, ignore agility, and ignore partial cover — every model in the 2" blast goes straight to armour penetration. Full cover still stops fragments via a line-of-sight check taken from the token. `[RB pp.16, 17]` — confidence: verified
- Overwatch **drops the moment a unit takes damage**, so the grenade is the reliable tool for cracking a covered overwatching gunline. `[RB p.11]` — confidence: verified
- **Why it is good:** a squad can only buy two grenades total from 4 equipment points, so the answer exists but is scarce. That is tight design. `[RB p.16]` — confidence: draft

### Overwatch has real teeth
- Interrupts **seven** distinct enemy action types — movement, shooting, reloading, equipment use, melee, disengage, and escape — resolving before the enemy's declared action. `[RB pp.10, 11]` — confidence: verified
- **PASS is explicitly carved out** as the one action that does not trigger it, giving the opponent a legitimate way to bait or wait. `[RB p.4]` — confidence: verified
- **Unique-ish:** reactive fire is common; reactive fire that fires *before* a declared shooting action, and that is stripped by damage, is a sharper implementation than most. — confidence: draft

### Melee strength does three jobs from one number
- It is the attack dice count, the **defence dice count**, and the target number an opponent must beat on a D6 to break Melee Lock. `[RB p.13]` — confidence: verified
- **Why it is good:** one stat carries a unit's entire melee identity, so a reader learns melee once. Also means melee is opposed-roll interactive rather than a one-sided attack. `[RB p.21]` — confidence: verified

### Melee stickiness is two-tiered
- Inside the melee radius you can walk out for 1 AP. Once **bases touch (Melee Lock)** you cannot — only Disengage (1 AP, roll, failure hands the enemy a free attack at reduced melee strength) or Escape (2 AP, always leaves). `[RB p.15]` — confidence: verified
- A unit locked with several enemies must **beat every one of them** to break away. `[RB p.15]` — confidence: verified
- **Why it is good:** it gives melee a genuine commitment cost without a flat "no leaving" rule, and it makes base contact a meaningful player decision. — confidence: draft

### Contracts: a catch-up mechanic that creates information asymmetry
- Trailing by 1+ VP at end of round hands you a **secret** assassination target worth VP, payable even if the target dies to scenario effects or friendly fire. `[RB p.22]` — confidence: verified
- **Unique:** most catch-up mechanics hand out resources. This one hands out a **hidden objective**, which changes what the leading player has to fear without telling them what it is. — confidence: draft

### Casualties fund reliability
- A player gains **1 re-roll point every time one of their own units dies**, on top of 2 per round while the Leader lives. `[RB p.23]` — confidence: verified
- Re-rolls are firewalled off the initiative roll and event-card rolls, so losing cannot buy tempo — only accuracy. `[RB p.23]` — confidence: verified
- **Unique:** paying the losing player twice, in two different currencies, with a firewall on the one currency that would snowball. Deliberate and well-considered. — confidence: draft

### Ammunition as a visible board state
- Every weapon has finite ammo, tracked with a token next to the model; 0 ammo blocks shooting until a reload is paid in AP. `[RB p.9]` — confidence: verified
- **Why it is good:** it turns "hold this lane forever" into a rhythm, and because the token is public, it becomes a read the opponent can play against. — confidence: draft

### Free actions reward good positioning
- Doors, item pickup, and **medkit use all cost 0 AP** — but only within 1", and only once per activation. `[RB pp.17, 18]` — confidence: verified
- **Why it is good:** the AP budget is only 2, so zero-cost actions gated on proximity are effectively a positional bonus rather than a freebie. — confidence: draft

### Loot persistence
- Equipment dropped by a killed unit stays on the board and can be taken by **either side**. `[RB pp.16, 17]` — confidence: verified
- **Unique-ish:** scarcity (4 equipment points) plus persistence plus a 0 AP pickup makes a dead specialist's grenade a live objective. — confidence: draft

## Strong structural choices

- **Randomised objectives before deployment.** One D6 picks one of six capture-point layouts, so deployment is planned against known but unpredictable scoring geometry. `[RB p.27]` — confidence: verified
- **Deploy first, arm second.** Equipment points are spent *after* both squads are on the table, turning loadout into a response rather than a guess. `[RB p.2]` — confidence: verified
- **Contested points pay nobody.** A single late-arriving model can zero out an enemy's round of income, so denial is as valuable as capture. `[RB p.4]` — confidence: verified
- **Per-die criticals.** A 6 on the damage check deals critical damage instead of normal, and per-die results sum — simple to teach, genuinely swingy. `[RB pp.10, 18, 20]` — confidence: verified
- **A scenario where a draw kills both players.** The beta scenario's tie condition destroys everyone. Uncommonly bold, and it removes the incentive to play for a safe draw. `[RB p.26]` — confidence: verified

## Strong project choices (non-rules)

- **The full beta rulebook is free and public before the campaign** — complete core rules, two playable rosters, a scenario, and the map system. This is the reason an independent review is possible at all. `[RB pp.1–37]` — confidence: verified
- **Faction difficulty ratings published pre-launch**, so a new player can pick honestly. `[PL §02]` — confidence: draft
- **$1 refundable co-design tier** with votes on a new squad's theme and a seat in balance discussion, delivered free on every tier. `[PL §10]` — confidence: draft
- **STL tier is a headline, not a stretch goal**, with resin-optimised minis and FDM-optimised terrain. `[PL §09]` — confidence: draft

## Honest caveat for the review

Nothing above is validated at the table. This project has played **zero games** of The Warcode. Every "why it is good" is a read of the text, not a playtest finding. State that plainly.
