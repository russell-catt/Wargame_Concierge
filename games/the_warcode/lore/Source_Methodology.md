# Source Methodology

> **How to read the dossier**
>
> - The academy frame is not an omniscient narrator.
> - Faction documents are biased primary sources, not academy conclusions.
> - Attribution is evidence: who speaks, for whom, and under what conditions matters.
> - Contradictions are intelligence problems to preserve, not gaps to smooth over.
> - Mechanics always defer to **The-Warcode-Rulebook-V.0.8.9-F.pdf**.

## The academy frame

The Field Edition presents itself as pre-command instruction from a military academy. Its introduction says the information environment has been deliberately degraded and that no single source, including a friendly one, gives a complete political or ideological picture. It also says political, historical, and ideological inserts are primary-source documents rather than academy assessment. (`The-Warcode-Lorebook-Tactical-Doctrine-Field-Edition.pdf` — PDF p.3 — Section 1, “Information Environment” and “Source Methodology”.)

This creates two useful distinctions:

1. **Academy connective prose** establishes the dossier's working geography, calendar, epoch boundaries, and section framing.
2. **Inserted documents** preserve what a speaker, institution, or faction said—not automatic proof that every claim inside is true.

The academy itself never claims omniscience. Even its structural frame should be cited as the dossier's account, not treated as knowledge from outside the setting.

## Evidence ladder

### 1. Academy structural statements

Use for the system layout, calendar, named epochs, and introductions. Describe these as academy framing.

### 2. Records and dated instruments

Ultimatums, manifests, internal memoranda, intercepted transmissions, and authenticated images are evidence that a document or observation exists. Separate the record from the claims and intentions inside it.

### 3. Faction-authored primary sources

Speeches, sermons, interviews, press statements, and internal analyses express institutional interests. Label the author and affiliation:

- Dominium devotional and public material frames obedience and isolation as protection.
- Protagen public material frames science, autonomy, and open communication as liberation.
- MDR public material frames unity and clone continuity as protection from Dominium.

### 4. Hostile-source assessments

A faction may provide useful intelligence about an enemy while also having reasons to exaggerate or dehumanize it. Examples include Dominium's assessment of MDR Executive Unit cohesion and its later combat profile of Ulfari fighters. Report these as hostile-source observations, not neutral species or faction psychology. (Lorebook PDF pp.34, 38 — Sections 6–7.)

### 5. Named scholarship and commentary

Historians, analysts, lecturers, bloggers, and institutional researchers offer interpretations. Preserve their affiliation and distinguish evidence from argument. “Independent” in a title or affiliation is a self-description, not proof of neutrality.

### 6. Project interpretation

These shipping pages use cautious constructions:

- “the academy frames”
- “the internal file records”
- “the faction claims”
- “the hostile briefing assesses”
- “the sources conflict”
- “this project interprets”

Inference never becomes canon merely because it resolves a contradiction neatly.

## Case study: first contact

The Erebus-7 file is unusually probative because Dominium's internal report conflicts with its later public line. It records a non-hostile image, identifies the ideological threat to Blackout, and issues suppression and escalation orders. (Lorebook PDF pp.35–37 — Section 7, “Internal Analysis — First Contact Event,” parts I–V.)

That supports a comparison, not a totalizing conclusion:

- the internal record undercuts an unqualified sudden-Ulfari-attack story;
- the directive proves that orders were issued, not that every order succeeded;
- later hostile observations indicate real violence but do not establish who began every clash;
- no Ulfari-authored document appears in the dossier, so their motives remain unknown.

The internal file remains a Dominium source selected by an unknown compiler. Its conflict with Dominium's public address increases its evidentiary value without making it infallible. (Lorebook PDF p.39 — Section 7, “Unite or Perish”.)

## Source-affiliation cautions

- The page-3 visual legend does not survive text extraction reliably; use written attributions under passages rather than guessing from icon placement.
- The Golden Age section includes a retrospective source dated year 2001, which falls in the Blackout epoch; placement does not make it contemporary testimony. (Lorebook PDF p.17 — Section 3.5, “Freedom to Leave”.)
- Claims about Martin's betrayal and Purification come from Protagen and MDR voices and lack neutral corroboration in this edition.
- MDR analysis of the Sed Festival raises a concern but does not establish mind control as fact.
- Dominium's origin remains unknown.

## Mechanics firewall

The lorebook supplies narrative context only. Its final section sends field execution and squad-level command to the accompanying rulebook. All action points, profiles, weapons, abilities, morale, scenarios, and timing use **The-Warcode-Rulebook-V.0.8.9-F.pdf** and the [`rules overview`](../rules/Overview.md). (`The-Warcode-Lorebook-Tactical-Doctrine-Field-Edition.pdf` — PDF p.40 — Section 8, “Tactical Application”.)
