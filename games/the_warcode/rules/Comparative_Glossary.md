# Comparative Glossary — The Warcode vs That other game

**Unofficial and unauthorized.** Personal learning snapshot of beta **V.0.8.7-F** (2026-08-23).

For each term: rulebook definition (cited) → one-sentence agentic commentary → one bracketed bridge to **That other game** / **Murder Platoon**.

---

## Round structure

### Round
**Definition:** One Initiative Phase + one Tactical Phase, then end-of-round abilities, scenario/Protocol effects, and VP calculation.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.3 — "GAME PHASES"  
**Agentic commentary:** Fixed-length campaigns of rounds make every activation a scarce resource, not an open-ended slog.  
(This seems related to Turning Points in That other game.)

### Initiative Phase
**Definition:** Roll a D6; highest roll activates first that round; round 1 also sets deployment order; ties re-roll.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.3 — "INITIATIVE PHASE"  
**Agentic commentary:** Initiative is a pure coin-flip each round rather than a fixed player order.  
(This seems related to determining who activates first within a Turning Point in That other game.)

### Tactical Phase
**Definition:** Players alternate activating units and spending Action Points.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.3 — "TACTICAL PHASE"  
**Agentic commentary:** This is the entire “play the game” window — almost all decisions live here.  
(This seems related to the Firefight Phase in That other game.)

### Activation / Activation token
**Definition:** Alternating one-unit activations; flip the token after acting; flip all back at round start.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.4 — "UNIT ACTIVATION"  
**Agentic commentary:** Tokens make “already activated” physically obvious and prevent accidental double-taps.  
(This seems related to ready/activated operative status in That other game.)

### Action Points (AP)
**Definition:** Currency for actions during an activation; typically **2 AP** per activation.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.4–5 — "ACTION POINTS (AP)"  
**Agentic commentary:** Two AP forces hard tradeoffs between moving, shooting, and reloading in one activation.  
(This seems related to Action Point Limit (APL) on operatives in That other game.)

### Pass
**Definition:** Skip the rest of the activation without performing actions; does not trigger Overwatch.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.4 — "UNIT ACTIVATION"  
**Agentic commentary:** Passing is the safe way to bank initiative without feeding reaction fire.  
(This seems related to forgoing remaining actions on an operative in That other game.)

## Scoring

### Victory Points (VP)
**Definition:** Scored from controlling map points and completing contracts; most VP at end wins.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.4–5 — "VICTORY POINTS (VP)"  
**Agentic commentary:** Standing on footprints matters as much as deleting models.  
(This seems related to mission scoring / Crit Op VP in That other game.)

### Contract
**Definition:** When behind on VP, draw a secret contract naming an enemy unit; eliminate it for bonus VP.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.22 — "CONTRACTS"  
**Agentic commentary:** Contracts are a designed comeback valve, not a participation trophy.  
(This seems related to Tac Op / secondary objective pressure in That other game.)

## Unit attributes

### Health (HP)
**Definition:** How much damage a unit can take before removal.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.6 — "HEALTH"  
**Agentic commentary:** Health is a simple wound track without separate “injured” breakpoints in the core text.  
(This seems related to Wounds on an operative datacard in That other game.)

### Agility
**Definition:** Defense characteristic used when being shot or struck; cover can raise it (cap noted in rules).  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.6 — "AGILITY"  
**Agentic commentary:** Agility is the “to-hit against me” number attackers must beat.  
(This seems related to Defence / Save interactions in That other game, though the dice chain differs.)

### Armor
**Definition:** Base value used with weapon Armor Penetration to resolve damage through.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.6 — "ARMOR"  
**Agentic commentary:** Armor is a penetration threshold, not a flat damage reduction table.  
(This seems related to Save / Armour characteristics in That other game.)

### Movement Range
**Definition:** Inches of movement per Move action (profiles vary Slow / Standard / Fast).  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.7 — "MOVEMENT"  
**Agentic commentary:** Movement cost in AP means repositioning competes with shooting every activation.  
(This seems related to Move / Dash distances in That other game.)

## Ranged combat

### Ammunition
**Definition:** Finite shots; empty weapons need a Reload action costing AP.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.8 — "AMMUNITION"  
**Agentic commentary:** Ammo scarcity is a first-class resource, not flavor text.  
(This seems related to limited special ammo or equipment uses in That other game, though Murder Platoon usually does not meter every rifle shot.)

### Reload
**Definition:** Spend AP to restore ammunition on a weapon.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.8 — "ACTION POINTS (AP) FOR RELOADING"  
**Agentic commentary:** Reloading on the clock is a tempo tax your opponent can punish.  
(This seems related to spending actions to rearm or use equipment in That other game.)

### Armor Penetration
**Definition:** Modifier applied when rolling to defeat target Armor.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.9 — "ARMOR PENETRATION"  
**Agentic commentary:** High AP weapons exist to crack hard targets, not just to inflate damage numbers.  
(This seems related to AP / piercing weapon rules in That other game.)

### Damage / Critical Damage
**Definition:** Normal and elevated damage results from successful attacks.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.9 — "DAMAGE" / "CRITICAL DAMAGE"  
**Agentic commentary:** Crits are a second damage band, so swinginess is baked into the attack profile.  
(This seems related to Normal / Critical Damage on weapons in That other game.)

### Overwatch
**Definition:** A stance allowing reaction fire under stated conditions during the opponent’s activation.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.10–11 — "OVERWATCH"  
**Agentic commentary:** Overwatch rewards positioning and punishes careless approaches through open ground.  
(This seems related to Overwatch / Guard-style reaction mechanics in That other game.)

### Cover (partial / full)
**Definition:** Terrain that modifies Agility or line of sight when intervening or occupied per the cover rules.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — cover sections in extract  
**Agentic commentary:** Cover is an Agility conversation more than a separate “light/heavy” keyword soup.  
(This seems related to Cover / Obscured tests in That other game.)

### Shooting through friendly units
**Definition:** Rules constrain firing through allies; check the worked example before assuming LOF.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.12 — "SHOOTING THROUGH FRIENDLY UNITS"  
**Agentic commentary:** Friendly bodies can block your own shooting plans.  
(This seems related to intervening models / visibility through friendlies in That other game.)

## Melee

### Melee Lock
**Definition:** Units in melee contact are locked; special rules (e.g. some abilities) can force lock within 1".  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.14–15 — "MELEE LOCK"  
**Agentic commentary:** Lock is sticky — you do not casually stroll away mid-fight.  
(This seems related to Engagement Range / being in combat in That other game.)

### Engage
**Definition:** Action to enter melee range / initiate melee interaction.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.14 — "ENGAGE"  
**Agentic commentary:** Engage is the intentional “I want this fight” button.  
(This seems related to Charge / Fight initiation in That other game.)

### Disengage / Escape from Melee Lock
**Definition:** Actions and tests to leave lock under the disengage/escape rules.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.15 — "DISENGAGING" / "ESCAPING"  
**Agentic commentary:** Leaving combat is a mechanic with failure risk, not a free walk.  
(This seems related to Fall Back / Retire options in That other game.)

### Melee Strength
**Definition:** Dice pool for melee attacks and related disengage numbers.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — melee attributes  
**Agentic commentary:** Melee Strength is both offense and the number you often need to beat to leave.  
(This seems related to Attack Dice / Fight profiles in That other game.)

## Equipment and map

### Equipment points
**Definition:** After deployment, spend equipment points to arm units before the game starts.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.2 — "DISTRIBUTE EQUIPMENT"  
**Agentic commentary:** Loadout is a post-deploy decision, so you can react to the opponent’s placement.  
(This seems related to equipment selection / universal equipment in That other game.)

### Grenade
**Definition:** Throwable equipment with a worked example for placement and effect.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.16 — "GRENADE"  
**Agentic commentary:** Grenades are scarce tools, not spamable free actions.  
(This seems related to grenade equipment in That other game.)

### Medkit
**Definition:** Healing/support equipment per the medkit rules.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.17 — "MEDKIT"  
**Agentic commentary:** Medkits create a heal-vs-pressure subplot around wounded units.  
(This seems related to medikit / stim equipment in That other game.)

### Item Pickup
**Definition:** Loot gear dropped by eliminated units; friend or foe may claim it.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.17 — "ITEM PICKUP"  
**Agentic commentary:** The board becomes an economy of dropped tools after the first kills.  
(This seems related to picking up objectives or rare mission items in That other game, though Murder Platoon rarely lets you steal the enemy’s gun mid-mission.)

### Door (open / close / block)
**Definition:** Map objects you interact with; doors can block movement and lines of fire.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.18 — "OPENING AND CLOSING DOORS"  
**Agentic commentary:** Doors are active tactical pieces, not static scenery.  
(This seems related to hatch/door terrain interactions in That other game.)

### Re-roll
**Definition:** Spend re-roll points (Leaders grant 2 per round while alive) to re-roll dice.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.22 — "RE-ROLL"  
**Agentic commentary:** Re-rolls are a limited bank, so Leaders are economy pieces as well as fighters.  
(This seems related to Command Point spends / re-roll ploys in That other game.)

## Protocols and scenario

### Scenario
**Definition:** Pre-game card setting objectives, victory conditions, and special rules.  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.2 — "READ THE SCENARIO"  
**Agentic commentary:** Always read the scenario before arguing about “default” victory conditions.  
(This seems related to mission / Crit Op cards in That other game.)

### Protocol Card
**Definition:** Event-style cards that apply room or board-wide effects (often end of round).  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — Protocol Cards — via OCR (`protocol_cards.ocr.txt`)  
**Agentic commentary:** Protocols are the “board fights back” layer that changes optimal positioning each round.  
(This seems related to mission event twists or Tac Op environmental pressure in That other game.)

### Protocol "Magnet" / "Total Magnet"
**Definition:** Movement −3" for units starting a Move in the affected room(s).  
Source: via OCR — Protocol Cards  
**Agentic commentary:** Magnet turns a chase into a slog and favors already-positioned gunlines.  
(This seems related to difficult terrain / movement penalties in That other game.)

### Protocol "Hunt" / "Total Hunt"
**Definition:** End of round: full-health units in the room take 3 damage.  
Source: via OCR — Protocol Cards  
**Agentic commentary:** Hunt punishes pristine units that camp a room without taking chips first.  
(This seems related to end-of-Turning-Point residual damage hazards in narrative missions of That other game.)

### Protocol "Electricity" / "Total Electricity"
**Definition:** End of round: roll D6 per unit in room; 3 or less → 3 damage.  
Source: via OCR — Protocol Cards  
**Agentic commentary:** Electricity is a random attrition tax on crowded rooms.  
(This seems related to hazardous area effects in That other game.)

### Protocol "Silence" / "Total Silence"
**Definition:** Units in the room cannot use ranged weapons.  
Source: via OCR — Protocol Cards  
**Agentic commentary:** Silence flips a gunfight into a melee problem overnight.  
(This seems related to “no shooting” mission constraints in That other game.)

### Protocol "Poison" / "Total Poison"
**Definition:** End of round: all units in the room take 2 damage.  
Source: via OCR — Protocol Cards  
**Agentic commentary:** Poison is reliable chip damage that forces exits or medkits.  
(This seems related to toxic/hazardous terrain damage in That other game.)

## Force vocabulary

### Squad
**Definition:** The eight-unit force a player fields.  
Source: marketing + team lists pp.33–36  
**Agentic commentary:** Fixed eight is the entire force size conversation — no points shopping list.  
(This seems related to a fixed Murder Platoon roster size in That other game.)

### Unit
**Definition:** One model with a profile, weapons, and optional special ability.  
Source: team lists / activation rules  
**Agentic commentary:** “Unit” here means a single operative-scale piece, not a multi-model 40K unit.  
(This seems related to an operative in That other game.)

### Leader
**Definition:** Keyword granting 2 re-roll points at the start of each round while the Leader lives.  
Source: team lists (e.g. Commander Rickman, Soul Eater)  
**Agentic commentary:** Losing your Leader is an economy crash as well as a body loss.  
(This seems related to Leader / KEYWORD operatives in That other game.)

### Sniper (special ability)
**Definition:** When this unit shoots, the target’s Agility is reduced by 1 (Shade).  
Source: The Warcode Rulebook V.0.8.7-F.pdf — p.36 — Ulfari Shade  
**Agentic commentary:** Sniper is a soft AP-to-Agility cheat that makes elite shots more reliable.  
(This seems related to marksman / precision shooting rules in That other game.)
