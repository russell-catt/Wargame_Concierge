# The Warcode vs That other game

**`confidence: draft`** — teaching comparison for personal learning. **Not endorsed by RedMakers or Gamefound.**

If you already play **Murder Platoon** (That other game's squad skirmish mode) or small-scale **Warhammer 40,000**, this page maps the same mental models onto The Warcode without pretending the two games are interchangeable.

---

## Comparison at a glance

| Axis | The Warcode (beta v0.8.7-F) | That other game (Murder Platoon mental model) |
|------|----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **Scale** | Fixed **8 models** per player; **33" × 24"** board | Typically **6–10 operatives**; killzone-sized board (~22" × 30" or similar) |
| **Length** | **Four rounds** in bundled scenarios; tight clock | Mission-driven; often **3–4 turning points** with longer clock on some ops |
| **Activation** | **Alternating single-unit** activation each Tactical Phase | **Alternating activation** of individual operatives (similar rhythm, different action economy) |
| **Action economy** | **2 AP per unit per round** — move, shoot, reload, overwatch, melee, equipment | **Action / energy / AP pools** vary by operative and turning point; generally more actions spread across the squad |
| **Scoring** | **VP from map control** (1" radius, uncontested) + **Contracts** when behind | Primary / tac ops, crit op cards, kill/mark objectives — mission package varies |
| **Resources** | **4 equipment points** (grenades / medkits); **ammo + reload**; **re-roll points** from Leader + casualties | Equipment, ploys, faction rules, CP — richer stratagem layer |
| **Comeback** | Trailing by **1+ VP** draws a secret **Contract** to eliminate a named enemy unit | Score gap and mission type drive comeback; no direct Contract analogue |
| **Models / list-building** | **No points** — pick the published 8-unit roster | Points or fixed team lists depending on mission and edition |

---

## Scale and table footprint

The Warcode standardises on a **33" × 24"** playing surface with scenario-printed deployment zones. Murder Platoon killzones are a different footprint; if you proxy at home, scale terrain toward Warcode's rectangle rather than forcing a killzone map unchanged.

Both games sit in the **skirmish band**: a handful of models, inches matter, cover is load-bearing. The Warcode roster is **slightly larger** (eight bodies fixed) than a typical six-operative Murder Platoon team, so lanes feel a bit more crowded and screening lines matter sooner.

---

## Game length and structure

Warcode games are **round-bounded**. Each round: Initiative Phase (D6) → Tactical Phase (alternating activations) → end-of-round VP and effects. The bundled *Core of the Machine* scenario adds a **countdown tie-break** — equal VP at game end can mean both squads lose.

Murder Platoon missions often stretch across **multiple turning points** with changing priority rules. Expect Warcode to feel **more clock-compressed**: fewer total activations per player across the whole match, so each AP spend carries more weight.

---

## Activation and tempo

The closest bridge for Murder Platoon players: **I activate one guy, you activate one guy**. That rhythm is familiar.

The divergence is **what one activation buys**. In Warcode, an activated unit has **2 AP** and a menu of actions (move, shoot, reload, overwatch, melee, engage, disengage, equipment, interact, pass). You are not managing a shared squad-wide pool for the whole turning point — you are finishing **one model's turn** before handing initiative back.

**Passing** is explicit and does **not** trigger Overwatch — a defensive tempo tool Murder Platoon handles differently.

---

## Scoring and win conditions

Murder Platoon usually scores through a **mission card stack** (primary, tac, crit, nemesis variants). Warcode scenarios specify **VP tokens on the map** and optional elimination / narrative win lines.

Core Warcode control rule (paraphrase): a unit within **1 inch** of a VP token with **no enemy in that radius** at end of round scores it; contested if both sides present.

**Contracts** are Warcode-specific comeback: if you trail by **1+ VP** at end of a round, draw a secret card naming one unit from the opponent's faction roster; eliminate that unit for bonus VP. The beta worked example uses **Shade** as an Ulfari contract target.

---

## Resources: equipment, ammo, re-rolls

| Resource | Warcode | Murder Platoon analogue |
|----------|---------|-------------------------|
| Pre-game gear | **4 equipment points** → grenades and/or medkits | Universal equipment, faction gear, pre-mission picks |
| Shooting | **Ammunition counters**; **reload** costs AP | Ammo / action rules vary by weapon and edition |
| Dice fixes | **Re-roll points**: 2/round while Leader lives; +1 when your unit dies | Ploys, faction abilities, crit op effects |
| Faction layer | Statlines + a few weapon riders; Protocol Cards (beta OCR pending) | Army rules, ploys, tac ops, team rules |

Warcode's resource layer is ** thinner by design** — less stratagem deck, more statline and positioning.

---

## Comeback mechanics

Murder Platoon comeback often lives in **mission design** and **score swings on late turning points**. Warcode bakes in **Contracts** when behind on VP, plus **death-driven re-roll income** (+1 re-roll point immediately when your unit is killed). Killing the enemy **Leader** shuts off their **2 re-roll points per round** — a swing as big as any single model loss.

---

## Models and collection expectations

| Topic | Warcode | Murder Platoon |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| List building | **Fixed roster** — eight named units per faction | Roster construction within points / limits |
| Factions in beta | **Protagen Marines** + **Ulfari** only | Many teams in current edition |
| Proxy friendliness | **Excellent** — see [`Proxy_Play_at_Home.md`](Proxy_Play_at_Home.md) | Already proxy-friendly at skirmish scale |

---

## Which game when?

- **Murder Platoon** when you want deep mission variety, faction ploys, and a mature team catalogue.
- **The Warcode** when you want a **fast, fixed-roster duel** with AP-per-model decisions and a flatter rules surface — good for a father–son table night with proxies.
