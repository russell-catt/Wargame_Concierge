# Oath of Moment - the Space Marine army rule

Once per turn you point at one enemy unit and say "that one". For the rest of the round, your whole army hits it better. That is the entire faction rule, and it is free.

**Where to check it:** your own Space Marines faction pack, `C:\Personal\40K\rules\eng_22-07_warhammer_40,000_faction_pack_space_marines.pdf` (Version 1.1, legal from 22 July 2026). The **Rules Updates** section near the back rewrites this rule, so the version printed in an older Codex is out of date. Read on **2026-08-16**.

---

## What the rule does

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Who gets it** | Every model in your army with the Oath of Moment ability - which, in an Adeptus Astartes army, is effectively everything |
| **When you pick** | At the **start of your Command phase**. One enemy unit |
| **How long it lasts** | Until the start of your **next** Command phase - so it covers your whole turn *and* your opponent's turn |
| **What it costs** | Nothing. No CP, no roll, no restriction on which enemy unit you choose |

Against your chosen target, every attack your army makes gets:

1. **Re-roll the Hit roll.** Not "re-roll 1s" - re-roll any miss, shooting and melee alike.
2. **Add 1 to the Wound roll**, *if* your army passes the purity condition below.

Two of the three steps of the attack sequence, improved, army-wide, for free. That is a very large rule.

**Wound band reminder:** +1 to Wound shifts the Core **05.02** S vs T band one step easier when it applies — keep [`../../rules/Wound_Roll_Reference.md`](../../rules/Wound_Roll_Reference.md) at the table.

---

## The condition most people get wrong

The **+1 to Wound** is not automatic. From the faction pack's Rules Updates, you get it only when **both** of these are true:

- You are using a **Codex: Space Marines Detachment**, and
- Your army includes **no** Blood Angels, Dark Angels, Deathwatch or Space Wolves units - and none of the units listed in those factions' own Munitorum Field Manual sections.

In practice: **a single Blood Angels or Dark Angels model in your list downgrades your army rule for the entire game.** If the son's collection has a Death Company marine or a Deathwing Terminator painted in with the rest of the force, that is a real decision, not a paint-scheme detail. Field them because you want them, not by accident.

The faction pack's FAQ also settles which detachments qualify: **every** detachment printed in Codex: Space Marines, and **every** detachment included in the Space Marines Faction Pack. [Gladius Task Force](Gladius_Task_Force.md) is one of them, so the learning list keeps the full rule.

---

## Three things the rule lets you do that look illegal

All three are confirmed in the faction pack's FAQ section, read 2026-08-16:

- **You can name a unit sitting inside a transport.** The passengers are a legal target even though you cannot shoot them.
- **You can name a unit still in Reserves**, off the table entirely. If you know the Terminators are deep-striking in this round, oath them at the start of the Command phase and be ready.
- **You do not have to be able to see it, reach it, or intend to shoot it.** Selection has no range or visibility requirement.

That last point is why the rule rewards planning. You are not choosing a shooting target, you are choosing *this round's answer*.

---

## How to choose, as a beginner

The instinct is to oath the scariest thing on the table. That is often wrong. Ask three questions in this order:

1. **What am I actually going to shoot this turn?** The rule is worth nothing on a unit you never attack. Oath the unit your guns are already pointed at.
2. **What is the hardest thing to hurt that I still have to hurt?** Re-rolling hits plus +1 to wound is worth most against a target you were going to struggle with - a tough vehicle, a high-save elite squad. Against a screen of cheap bodies you were going to kill anyway, it is wasted.
3. **What will still be there on their turn?** The oath lasts through your opponent's turn, so it also improves your Fight-phase swings and any overwatch. A unit that is about to charge you is a legitimate pick even if you cannot shoot it now.

**A concrete habit:** decide your oath target *after* you have looked at the board and before you have moved anything. It is the first real decision of your turn and it should be made deliberately, out loud, with a token placed next to the target model.

---

## Why this changes how you play

**Concentration beats spread.** Because the bonus applies only to one enemy unit, an army that splits fire across four targets throws most of its army rule away. Space Marines want to pick one thing per round and delete it. This is the opposite instinct to a horde army, and it is the single biggest adjustment for a new player.

Three habits follow:

- **Sequence your shooting.** Fire everything at the oath target first. Only when it is dead do you spill over into the rest.
- **Do not oath out of fear.** A unit you cannot reach this turn does not become reachable because you named it.
- **Re-pick every single Command phase.** The oath is not permanent and there is no cost to changing it. Forgetting to re-select is the same mistake as a Necron forgetting to reanimate: a free, powerful ability quietly skipped.

---

## The one thing to get right in your first game

Put a physical token on the oath target. Every Command phase, move it. The rule is easy to understand and remarkably easy to forget, because nothing in the turn sequence stops you if you skip it - and unlike most missed abilities, you cannot claim it later.
