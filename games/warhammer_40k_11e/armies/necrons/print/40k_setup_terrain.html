Setup & terrain — first game

Shortcut for a kitchen-table first game, then the terrain rules that matter most. Core IDs are pointers.

First-game setup shortcut

  1. Use a small table and a small points limit (e.g. ~250).
  2. Skip Secondary Missions. Score only “who controls more objectives at the end of each battle round”.
  3. Place three objectives on a centre line (not a full event layout).
  4. Deploy everything — no reserves for game one.
  5. Play three battle rounds, not five.

Add one piece of the real sequence back per game.

Regions & objectives

RegionWhat it is
Deployment zonesWhere each side sets up, wholly within
No Man’s LandEverything in neither zone — where the game is decided
TerritoryHalf of the board containing your deployment zone
  • Objectives are usually terrain areas (14.01) — the terrain footprint is the objective; stand inside to be in range.
  • Only when not a terrain area: physical objective marker (40 mm; range 3" horizontal / 5" vertical).
  • Control (14.02): sum OC of models in range; higher total controls; re-checked every phase. Mission card says when you score VP. Not Kill Team’s 1" control.
  • Force Disposition must be on the list at Muster — matching picks each Primary. See Chapter Approved Force Dispositions 2-pager.

Terrain areas come first

Agree terrain areas out loud before the first model moves. A terrain area is a defined region; a terrain feature is the physical model (ruin, barricade, crater).

  • Objectives are usually terrain areas.
  • Cover depends on being inside a terrain area (not “behind a wall” alone).
  • Hidden and Obscuring operate on terrain areas.

Three categories

CategoryTypicalEffect
ExposedCraters, debrisMove freely; least protection
LightBarricades, low wallsMove freely; area is obscuring
DenseRuins, buildings, woodsBlocks larger models; Solid; shapes the game
Benefit of Cover (13.08): worsens the attacker’s Ballistic Skill by 1. It is not a save bonus. If you learned older editions, unlearn the save bonus.

Visibility (pointers)

  • Line of sight — 06.01.
  • Hidden (13.09) — INFANTRY / BEASTS / SWARM in a Dense area, if the unit did not shoot this or last turn; detection usually 15".
  • Obscuring (13.10) — Light/Dense areas can block LOS across intervening areas.
  • Solid — Dense features: no LOS through ground-floor gaps within 3" of ground.

How much terrain (learning)

For a learning game: four to six substantial pieces spread across the board, with at least a couple you cannot see through. Dense terrain balances shooting vs melee.

  • Leave room inside each terrain area for models to stand (cover / Hidden / terrain objectives).
  • Leave lanes for Vehicles/Monsters near edges.

Setup checklist

  • ☐ Terrain areas placed and agreed first
  • ☐ Features placed; every piece called Exposed / Light / Dense out loud
  • ☐ Objectives placed; clear which areas are objectives
  • ☐ Enough Dense to break long fire lanes
  • ☐ Dice, tape measure, datasheets ready
