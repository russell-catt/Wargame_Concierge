# Team Rule Guide — Obelisk Node Matrix

The Canoptek Circle's whole game plan lives in one faction rule. Learn this page and you know how to play the team; everything else (ploys, equipment) just leans on it.

**Confidence: draft.** Paraphrased from the living Wahapedia Canoptek Circle page, retrieved **2026-08-17**. The local team PDF (`eng_29-04_kill_team_team_rules_canoptek_circle-*.pdf`) has not been opened yet — cross-check before a tournament.

---

## The core idea

You control **three Obelisk Node markers**. Where you put them, and how close together they are, decides how strong your team is in that part of the killzone. Think of them as three portable "home turf" beacons — place them well, keep them together, and your Canoptek constructs fight better inside the zone they create.

---

## Placing and moving nodes

| When | What you do |
|------|-------------|
| **First turning point** | As a Strategic Gambit, place all three Obelisk Node markers anywhere wholly within your own territory. |
| **Every turning point after the first** | As a Strategic Gambit, you can move each node up to 3" horizontally. You do not have to move all three, or any of them. |
| **Mid-activation nudge** | A Firefight Ploy (see below) lets you shuffle nodes around during an activation instead of using a Strategic Gambit — useful when you need the Matrix to react to how the enemy actually moved. |

**Habit:** decide your node triangle at deployment with the whole game in mind, not just turn one. Nodes are slow to reposition (3" a turn from the Strategic Gambit) — you are steering a static installation, not a mobile one.

---

## What a node does on its own

Independent of the Matrix, each node **controls any objective/mission marker within 1" of it that no enemy operative contests** — the game treats a node like a friendly operative for control purposes only. If two players' nodes would both try to control the same marker, neither one does.

**Habit:** a node sitting on an objective can hold it for you without tying down an operative there. This is a big part of why the archetype is Security/Recon — you can garrison ground with furniture instead of models.

---

## Forming the Matrix

Whenever **two of your nodes are within 6" of each other**, the space between them (and the two markers themselves) becomes an **Obelisk Node Matrix** — height in the killzone does not matter, it is a flat measurement. If **all three** nodes are mutually within 6", the three linked areas combine into one larger Matrix.

**Habit:** a tight triangle (all three within 6" of each other) is the strongest shape because it gives you one connected zone instead of two separate strips. A loose spread trades Matrix size for battlefield coverage — there is a real trade-off here, not just "always cluster them."

---

## What the Matrix does for you

Any friendly Canoptek Circle operative standing inside the Matrix gets, at a habit level:

- **More reliable shooting** — its weapons gain an Accurate bonus, meaning some of its attack dice can be banked as automatic hits instead of rolled.
- **+1 to its APL, capped at 3** — effectively an extra action's worth of tempo that activation.

**Habit:** the Matrix rewards operatives that fight *inside* it, not operatives that merely stand near it. Route your Geomancer, Tomb Crawlers, and Macrocytes through the Matrix corridor on their way to a fight whenever the geometry allows it — it is close to a free upgrade for every action they take there.

## Strategy Ploys — one-line gist each

Used during the Strategy phase, spend CP as normal. Full effect text lives in the team PDF/Wahapedia — do not rely on this table alone at the table.

| Ploy | Gist |
|------|------|
| **Hypershielding** | Lets a Matrix-adjacent Canoptek re-roll a chosen defence-die result when it's shot at. |
| **Cryptogravitic Repulsion** | Makes the first enemy move into your Matrix cost the enemy extra distance, once per action. |
| **Transdynamic Amplification** | Gives a shooting Canoptek's weapons a re-roll-style rules boost while the Matrix is involved. |
| **Souldrain** | Weakens the melee damage of enemies fighting inside, or against a defender inside, your Matrix. |

---

## Firefight Ploys — one-line gist each

Used during an operative's activation or counteraction.

| Ploy | Gist |
|------|------|
| **Nodal Response** | Lets you swap or re-trigger a Strategy Ploy mid-activation instead of only in the Strategy phase. |
| **Animate Obelisk Nodes** | Moves your nodes (up to 6" combined) *during* the Firefight phase instead of waiting for the next Strategic Gambit — your reactive-node tool. |
| **Shield Flare** | Once triggered, can shrug off a point of incoming damage on a Matrix-linked Canoptek. |
| **Sacrificial Thrall** | Lets a nearby Canoptek take a hit or a melee attack meant for your Geomancer. |

---

## Faction equipment — one-line gist each

Selected before the battle, one use each per battle unless the equipment says otherwise.

| Equipment | Gist |
|-----------|------|
| **Matrix Manipulator** | Once per battle, treats the Geomancer itself as a fourth node — useful to stretch the Matrix somewhere the three physical markers cannot reach. |
| **Nanoscarab Caskets** | Passive healing trickle for every Canoptek that activates. |
| **Awakened Obelisk Nodes** | Banks a few free uses of the Animate Obelisk Nodes ploy. |
| **Phase Shifter** | Defensive tech that blunts armour-piercing attacks against the Geomancer specifically. |

---

## Putting it together — a first-game plan

1. **Deploy with the triangle in mind.** Picture where you want the Matrix to sit for the whole game, not just turn one — you can only nudge nodes 3" a turn.
2. **Garrison an objective with a node**, not an operative, when you can — it frees that operative to do something else.
2. **Route fights through the Matrix.** A Tomb Crawler trading blows inside the zone is a materially better trade than the same fight outside it.
3. **Keep Nodal Response and Animate Obelisk Nodes in reserve** for the turn the enemy tries to route around your triangle — they are your two reactive tools.
