SECTION 3: HISTORY
CHRONOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK
Before you study history, you need to know how it's measured. All dates in this document use
the standard calendar anchored to Erda:
28-hour day, 39-day month, 12-month year, 468 days per year
Year Zero is the appearance of the city of Dominium on Erda's surface. Everything before that is
counted in negative years. Everything after — in the ﬁve epochs listed below. Learn them now.
Every section that follows assumes you know this structure.
EPOCH 0. THE INDUSTRIAL ERA (PRE-ZERO). Erda's indigenous industrial civilization.
Multiple nation-states, analog-mechanical technology, no digital systems. Duration unconﬁrmed.
Records fragmentary.
EPOCH 1. FOUNDATION (YEAR 0–238). Appearance of Dominium. A single city-state of
unknown origin, technologically superior, willing to trade but refusing external interference.
The rest of Erda remains industrial.
EPOCH 2. THE BURNING (YEAR 238–380). Global nuclear war among Erda's
nation-states. Dominium intervenes militarily, destroys nuclear production capability, consolidates
planetary control. 142 years, extraordinarily bloody.
EPOCH 3. THE GOLDEN AGE (YEAR 380–1648). Post-war reconstruction
and expansion. Dominium leads humanity into space. Colonization of planets, orbital cities,
deep-space stations out to the Abyron Belt. 1,268 years of stability and growth.
Longest peaceful period in recorded history.
EPOCH 4. THE BLACKOUT (YEAR 1648–2026). Dominium enforces system-wide
communications isolation. Civil war follows. Emergence of independent factions. 378 years
of conﬂict gradually stabilizing into an armed standoff.
EPOCH 5. THE ARRIVAL (YEAR 2026, PRESENT DAY). First conﬁrmed contact
with an external intelligence — the Ulfari. Current epoch. Consequences unfolding.

THE INDUSTRIAL ERA
At the time of Dominium's founding, humanity on Erda had reached an analog-mechanical
industrial stage: heavy combustion-driven industry, electromechanical infrastructure,
no digital systems. This is the baseline from which the entire theatre developed.
It was the age of molten steel and crackling radio static, of chemical plants stretching
across river deltas and mines bored so deep they touched the planet's heat.
Extraction and factory production had been scaled to staggering proportions — entire
regions existed solely to feed the furnaces of nations. And there were hundreds of
nations, each convinced its particular arrangement of power and labor was
the answer to the human condition.
They competed in ideology as ﬁercely as in industry — monarchies against republics,
collectivists against mercantilists, theocracies against secular unions — forging
alliances, breaking them, warring, trading, prospering, and warring again. But what
makes this period so remarkable is something that never happened: no information
revolution, no microelectronics, no digital shortcut. The analog ceiling was never
broken. And so, century after century of that boiling competition, hundreds of rival
states, each engineering its way through the same fundamental constraints, produced
a mechanical ingenuity almost impossible to catalogue.
The sheer variety of solutions they invented for the same problems remains,
even now, difﬁcult to fully comprehend.
["THE LONG FURNACE," DR. EMMERICH VAEL. KOVARA
UNIVERSITY PRESS.]

But what Dominium built around itself was never a simple buffer — it was a fully
functioning system. Three states were bound to it through formal alliance treaties,
two located directly on its borders and a third positioned at a short remove.
Calling them satellites would misread the arrangement, since each received
something valuable in exchange for its loyalty.
Dominium sold them military technology, though rarely anything that could be called
revolutionary. These were established engineering principles reﬁned past what any
rival state could manage on its own. Engines and mechanisms ran longer and more
efﬁciently while weighing considerably less than comparable hardware elsewhere,
and armor plating held up marginally better under ﬁre. Fuel and electricity were
included on the same terms, with everything priced low enough that no ally had
reason to look elsewhere.
In exchange, Dominium secured what its own territory could never supply in sufﬁcient
volume: raw mineral resources, drawn in enormous quantities from every corner of
Erda. Meeting that demand required sources beyond what a single state could offer,
and that is where the allies proved their worth: serving as intermediaries with the
outside world, building trade chains and proﬁting at every link. It made them wealthy,
powerful, and bound the allies to Dominium more tightly than any treaty ever could.
The conditions Dominium imposed in return were deceptively simple. Allies were
expected to maintain good relations, both with Dominium and with one another,
and to keep their own territories defended. Expansion beyond their own borders was
expressly prohibited. The moment a state showed the faintest interest in imperialism,
Dominium intervened directly in its internal politics without bothering with diplomatic
formalities.
The result was exactly what the whole arrangement had been designed
to achieve: the city was securely protected on the ground. As for the airspace,
even if the occasional aircraft slipped past the allies' perimeters, it made
no difference. No aircraft that entered Dominium's airspace ever came back out.

EPOCH 1. FOUNDATION (YEAR 0–238)
Dominium's ﬁrst conﬁrmed appearance on Erda is dated to year 0. A single city-state, origin
undetermined. Surviving records show a technological differential relative to the rest of Erda that
widened, not narrowed, across this epoch. Dominium's early expansion was not conquest.
It secured itself through trade and defensive alliance with its immediate neighbors, making
Dominium indispensable to the states closest to it while restricting all deeper contact with
the wider world. To states outside those alliances, Dominium registered as unremarkable:
stable, defended, non-expansionist. Surviving records from the period show no meaningful threat
assessment directed at Dominium. Attention was ﬁxed on conﬂicts among other nation-states.
This is operationally relevant. Sources from this epoch consistently underestimate the scale of
the technological gap Dominium already held. Read every non-Dominium account of this period
with that limitation in mind.

THE BURNING (YEAR 238–380)
The conﬂict began with the ﬁrst use of nuclear weapons on Erda and became the bloodiest war in
human history. It engulfed every continent and nearly every nation-state on the planet. In total,
the war lasted 142 years, claimed more than one billion lives, and inﬂicted catastrophic ecological
damage from which entire regions never recovered.
You will study three distinct phases:
1. The nuclear phase (9 years), during which warheads were produced and
employed at escalating rates.
2. The continental phase (56 years) of sustained large-scale conventional and
asymmetric warfare across all major landmasses.
3. The attrition phase (86 years), marked by recurring regional conﬂicts that ignited,
subsided, and reignited across devastated territories. Each phase produced different tactical
conditions.

SUBJUGATION (YEAR 247–303)
Dominium made a major strategic error, feeding technology to their allies drop by drop.
They should have committed everything at once and handed the allies weapons that
outclassed the enemy completely from day one. Feeding out technology in small doses
never got Dominium a fast, decisive result. It only nudged the allies' position forward,
a little at a time, while the enemy adapted around it.
Most importantly, handing out the technology that exceeds what the allies could
develop on their own would practically be magic to them — unreachable and
impossible to copy. Instead, by releasing low-level technology that ended up in the
hands of both allies and enemies, Dominium gave the opposition a reason to study it.
The enemy took the technology apart, trying to copy it. Some of it worked. Some of it
didn't. But even where they failed, they learned one thing: it was, in fact, possible.
Dominium should never have underestimated the enemy’s ability to learn.
This opposition was resourceful and motivated. Yes, they were always a step behind in
equipment, but they excelled in numbers, fought for their own survival, and drew hope
from every piece of technology they managed to reverse-engineer.
That hope kept them adapting. And that is why this war lasted as long as it did.

3-5.THE GOLDEN AGE (YEAR 380–1648)
After Dominium won the war for full control of Erda, centuries of growth and stability followed. The
Father and the Mother of Dominium became the planet's supreme authority, acting as guarantor of
planetary order. They functioned as a structure above any single nation, serving other states at
once as teacher, arbiter, and enforcer.
This epoch is the longest in recorded history. It was during this period that humanity made the leap
in technological development from the industrial era to the full settlement and colonization of the
